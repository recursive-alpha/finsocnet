### [BOTH] The 4 Non-Negotiable Principles of Professional Execution
- **Category:** `edge`
- **Instrument:** `futures`
- **Falsifiable Statement:** Trading edge is lost not from flawed analysis, but from execution failure; professional profitability requires strict adherence to four execution laws: sit on hands when no edge exists, step up without hesitation when edge triggers, trade the market rather than P&L, and learn from mistakes through systematic review.
- **Setup Criteria:** Execution protocol applied to all trades across all instruments
- **Holding Period:** Flexible
- **Statistical Evidence:** Execution discipline separates consistently profitable prop traders from chronic retail losers
- **Invalidation Risk:** Taking un-planned trades or hesitating on valid setups destroys expectancy
- **Verbatim Ground Truth:**
> "Number one, sit on your hands when you don't believe an opportunity is high probability... the hard part is trying not to act like an idiot in between them. Number two, step up when you believe it's time to do so... no fear, no hesitation. Number three, trade the market not your P&L. And number four, try to learn something from your trades."

### [BOTH] Institutional Liquidity Sweep & S/R Level Reclamation
- **Category:** `execution`
- **Instrument:** `futures`
- **Falsifiable Statement:** Price frequently spikes through obvious daily and 4-hour support/resistance levels to sweep retail stop orders and trap breakout participants; when price aggressively rejects the sweep and reclaims the level, entering in the direction of the reclamation with stops beyond the wick provides asymmetric edge.
- **Setup Criteria:** Key horizontal S/R level, violent liquidity sweep beyond level, swift rejection and candle close back inside structure
- **Holding Period:** Intraday to swing
- **Statistical Evidence:** Fading violent sweeps into key levels yields superior win rates and 3:1+ Risk:Reward
- **Invalidation Risk:** Price sustains momentum beyond the level without rejection
- **Verbatim Ground Truth:**
> "My edge lies not just in knowing what levels to trade... I started leaving orders in when price would collapse into a level very violently and I started getting more winning trades, more edge, making more money."

### [BOTH] Negative Visualization for Zero-Hesitation Execution
- **Category:** `risk_management`
- **Instrument:** `futures`
- **Falsifiable Statement:** Hesitation and fear of pulling the trigger stem from unwillingness to accept loss; mentally visualizing the trade stopping out and accepting the dollar loss before entering diminishes fear and allows flawless, detached execution.
- **Setup Criteria:** Pre-trade routine executed immediately prior to order submission
- **Holding Period:** Instant
- **Statistical Evidence:** Diminishes performance anxiety and prevents hesitation feedback loops
- **Invalidation Risk:** Trading with high emotional expectation leads to premature exits and micro-management
- **Verbatim Ground Truth:**
> "Before you pull the trigger, you see it stopping you out and you see the loss in your account so you imagine it, you make peace with it, and then you take the trade... the fear is diminished, you're literally lessening the mental impact of being wrong."

### [BOTH] The -2R Daily Drawdown Circuit Breaker
- **Category:** `risk_management`
- **Instrument:** `futures`
- **Falsifiable Statement:** Loss clusters trigger emotional deterioration and revenge trading; establishing a mandatory daily circuit breaker (e.g. stop trading upon reaching -2R loss for the day) halts capital bleeding and forces mental reset.
- **Setup Criteria:** Cumulative daily P&L reaches -2R
- **Holding Period:** Rest of the trading day
- **Statistical Evidence:** Eliminates catastrophic blow-up days that erase months of steady gains
- **Invalidation Risk:** Violating the circuit breaker leads to compounded tilt and severe drawdowns
- **Verbatim Ground Truth:**
> "You need to insert something into the process that will make the bad practice impossible. So for example, I personally have a rule: if I hit 2R down for the day, I stop trading on my swing strategy."

### [BOTH] Trade the Market, Not Your P&L or Emotional Expectations
- **Category:** `edge`
- **Instrument:** `futures`
- **Falsifiable Statement:** Evaluating open positions based on account balance, daily target needs, or past losses induces premature profit-taking or stubborn over-holding; active trade management must respond solely to structural chart targets and technical invalidation.
- **Setup Criteria:** Open position management
- **Holding Period:** Trade duration
- **Statistical Evidence:** Trading P&L causes profit giveback and stops winners from reaching natural targets
- **Invalidation Risk:** Holding past technical targets because of personal financial desires
- **Verbatim Ground Truth:**
> "The market is not the fact that you did your ass in the last three trades... The market doesn't care that you need it to move up 14R to get to a new account high. I cannot tell you how often I used to get in a trade, see it go to my next level, look at my P&L and go 'nah that's not enough for a day's work' and start looking for reasons to hold. All I did was keep giving back profit. You have to trade what you see in front of you."

### [BOTH] The Daily Trade Plan, Logging Missed Trades & The Demon Finder
- **Category:** `edge`
- **Instrument:** `futures`
- **Falsifiable Statement:** Traders suffer from recency bias by remembering only recent outcomes; recording planned trades, logging missed trades, and utilizing a Demon Finder diagnostic sheet systematically exposes behavioral flaws and eliminates recurring errors.
- **Setup Criteria:** Pre-market plan sheet filled daily, post-session Demon Finder review
- **Holding Period:** Daily review routine
- **Statistical Evidence:** Exposes subconscious trading flaws and removes execution leaks
- **Invalidation Risk:** Failing to log missed trades blinds traders to their own execution inconsistencies
- **Verbatim Ground Truth:**
> "You can use a Demon Finder to quickly identify bad practices and then the next thing you want to do is map out the situations in which bad practices occur... I had never noticed this because I never recorded my missed trades, so I was always at the mercy of recency bias."
